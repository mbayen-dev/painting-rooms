1. Your painting

Switch to Trace to mark the image. Use the other views to see the model.

2. Horizon and view

The horizon is the viewer's eye level: where the floor lines would meet if they ran to infinity. Drag the line in Trace, or use the slider.

A single painting cannot tell you how far away the viewer stood. Change the field of view and the room stretches or squeezes in depth while the view from the viewer's eye stays identical.

3. Scale

Pick something whose real height you know, such as a standing adult. Click where it touches the floor, then its top.

How the maths works
Focal length(width / 2) / tan(fov / 2)
Depth of a floor pointf · h / (row − horizon)
Height of a standing thing(base − top) · depth / f
Camera heightH · (base − horizon) / (base − top)

Everything rests on the floor being flat, things standing upright, and the viewer looking straight ahead. Where a painter bends those rules, the model will show it as a size or position that does not look right. That is a finding, not a failure.
