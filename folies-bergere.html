Bottles stand on the counter, so their bases give their depth. The barmaid's depth follows from her head height, which comes out at a natural 22 cm when she stands 2.05 m away. Eye height and counter height are assumptions; the room hangs together across a range of values.

The ledge is the best test. The gold strip under the mirror was solved from its own pixels at the mirror's distance, and lands within a centimetre of the counter height. The barmaid's eyes come out a little high for a woman of the period, so she may stand on a duckboard or Manet raised her.

Confidence

Measured Bottle positions and sizes, counter, mirror ledge, barmaid's head size, the man's reflected position.

Inferred Viewing distance, eye height, the gap between her and the mirror, the customer's position outside the right edge.

Invented The hall behind the viewer, the crowd, her hidden legs, the floor, the room on either side.

Open question. The reflected barmaid looks about as large as the real one. A mirror 20 cm behind her would make her reflection about 16 percent smaller. Either the glass is much closer than that or the reflection was painted by eye.
