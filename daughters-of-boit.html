What is the mirror reflecting?

The small glass on the back wall shows a bright window. It hangs on a wall facing the viewer, so a ray from the viewer reflects straight back across the room. Turn it to test other mounts.

In Fast preview the glass is a true planar reflection. In Path traced mode it reflects with full bounces.

Floor point depthf · h / (row − horizon)

The rug is a rectangle turned about 21° from the walls, so its two edges give two vanishing points at columns −215 and 5200. For perpendicular edges, f² equals the product of their distances from the centre, which gives 1800 px. The horizon and eye height come from placing Mary Louisa (8) and the elder sisters at their plausible heights on the floor: the viewer's eye is about as low as a child's.

Confidence

Measured Floor positions of the four girls, the vases, the rug outline, the left wall line.

Inferred Camera height, focal length, the depth of the walls and alcove, the cabinet and the red door.

Invented Light positions other than the direction of the far-left window, the ceiling and anything outside the frame, hidden bodies, the room to the right of the red door.

Review note. The focal length assumes the picture's centre is the viewer's axis. If the axis sits further right, f rises (about 2000 px at column 700), and the direction the mirror reflects changes. With a mirror hung flat on the back wall, a ray from a viewer left of the glass reflects to the viewer's right, not the far left. A far-left window only shows in the glass if the glass is turned toward the left (about 35 degrees) or the viewer's axis lies to the right of the glass. Use the mirror turn control to test.
