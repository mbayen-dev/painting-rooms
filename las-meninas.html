The vanishing point is where the right wall and ceiling lines meet, in the doorway. Floor depths use the hems of the dresses and the dog. Focal length cannot be measured from one vanishing point, so it is an assumption and the slider above shows what it changes.

The lamps are the best test. Each lamp was placed by a different pixel pair, and the model returns the same height and the same side offset for both. Doorway, mirror and picture sizes are all plausible for a 17th century palace room.

Confidence

Measured Vanishing point, lamps, doorway, mirror, back pictures, floor positions from skirt hems.

Inferred Viewing distance, back wall depth (8 m), camera height, the right wall and its windows, the size of the canvas.

Invented Left wall, rear sides of figures, hidden legs, the stairwell, everything behind the viewer.

Open question. Taken at face value, Velázquez comes out about 1.5 m tall. Either he is painted taller than the perspective allows, or his feet sit higher than the floor plane assumed here. The same stretch shows in the figures at the back. Painters of the period often adjusted scale for dignity.
